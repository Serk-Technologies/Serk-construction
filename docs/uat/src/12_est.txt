# 7. Business development, estimating and tendering (SRS 6.3)
@release R2

## Industry context
Contractors win work by **tendering**: the client issues drawings and a bill of quantities (BOQ); the contractor prices every line, adds overheads, risk and margin, and submits a sealed bid. Price too high and the bid is lost; price too low and the contractor wins a loss-making job. Good estimating therefore needs (1) a maintained **cost database** of material, labour and plant rates, (2) **unit-rate build-ups** that show how each rate was made, and (3) a way to reuse the winning estimate as the project's **baseline budget** so nobody has to type the numbers again.

Two controls matter here: a **submitted BOQ is frozen** (you cannot quietly change the numbers you bid), and an **approved BOQ becomes the budget through a controlled transition**, never by editing in place (EST-02, EST-07).

@term Unit rate = The price for one unit of an item of work, built up from labour, plant, material and subcontract costs plus overhead, risk and margin.
@term Take-off = Measuring quantities from drawings or a model to fill the BOQ.
@term Bid bond = A guarantee issued with a bid so the client is protected if the winner refuses to sign.
@term Addendum = A formal change issued by the client during the tender period.
@term Go/no-go = The decision whether a tender is worth bidding for.

@case T-EST-02 | A submitted BOQ is frozen; changing it needs a new version; approval is by another person | R1 | EST-02 | EST, QS
pre: BOQ version 1 for P-101 with at least 5 items (item, unit, quantity, rate, cost code).
1. As est.a map every BOQ item to an approved cost code, then submit version 1.
2. Try to change a quantity in the submitted version.
3. Create version 2 as a copy; change one rate; compare the two versions.
4. As est.a try to approve version 2; then as qs.a approve it.
=> Step 2 is refused; the message explains that submitted versions are immutable and that a new version is required.
=> The version comparison lists exactly the changed items, with old and new amounts and the difference.
=> Self-approval is refused; qs.a succeeds. All versions remain readable.
why: If a bid can be changed after submission, either the company cannot prove what it offered, or someone can alter it unseen.

@case T-EST-03 | Resource-based estimate: a unit rate built from labour, plant, material, productivity, overhead, risk and margin | R2 | EST-03 | EST
pre: Cost database rates: cement ETB 900 per bag, sand ETB 1,800 per m3, gravel ETB 2,200 per m3, labour ETB 56.25 per hour (day rate 450 / 8 hours), mixer and vibrator ETB 800 per hour. Policy: productivity factor 1.10 on labour only; overhead 8% and risk 2% on direct cost; margin 15% on the subtotal; the rate is rounded to 2 decimals before multiplying by quantity.
1. As est.a build the rate for 1 m3 of C-25 concrete: cement 7 bags, sand 0.45 m3, gravel 0.85 m3, water and admixture lump sum 150, labour 6 hours, plant 0.5 hour.
2. Apply the policy and read the resulting rate.
3. Price a BOQ line of 1,000 m3 with this rate.
=> Materials 9,130.00; labour 337.50, with productivity 371.25; plant 400.00; direct cost 9,901.25.
=> Overhead 792.10; risk 198.03 (198.025 shown to the rounding rule); subtotal 10,891.38; margin applied; **unit rate 12,525.08**.
=> The 1,000 m3 line amount is **12,525,080.00** (rate rounded first, then multiplied) and the rounding rule is visible.
=> Change the cement price in the database: a newly priced estimate changes, the already-approved version does not.
note: Tolerance of 0.01 is allowed on the intermediate lines; the final rate and line amount must match exactly. The tenant rounding policy is documented in the data sheet.
why: Tender rates must be explainable. When the client asks why a concrete rate is high, the estimator opens the build-up.

@case T-EST-07 | An approved BOQ becomes the baseline budget; it is never edited in place | R1 | EST-07, PCC-01 | QS, PM
pre: BOQ version 2 for P-101 is approved (T-EST-02).
1. As pm.a request "Create baseline budget from approved BOQ".
2. Open the budget: original budget lines per cost code and their BOQ item links.
3. Try to edit the original budget amount of a cost code.
4. Try to edit the approved BOQ.
=> The budget is created by a recorded transition, line by line traceable to BOQ items (a budget line shows its BOQ source).
=> Totals reconcile: sum of cost codes = 100,000,000 for P-101.
=> Steps 3 and 4 are refused. Changes can only be made through a budget revision or a new BOQ version (see T-PCC-01).
why: The original budget is the yardstick for the whole project. If it can be quietly adjusted, no overrun is ever visible.
