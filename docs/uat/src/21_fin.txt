# 16. Finance and accounting (SRS 6.13)
@release R1

## Industry context
The general ledger is the company's single book of record. Every business event (stock issued to a job, supplier invoice approved, IPC certified, payroll approved) produces a **journal**: a set of debit and credit lines that must be equal. Once **posted** a journal can never be changed; mistakes are fixed by a **reversing journal** so an auditor can always see what originally happened. Books are kept in monthly **accounting periods**; once a period is **closed**, nothing can be posted into it unless a senior person formally reopens it, and that act is itself audited.

Contractors have special finance needs: **retention and advances** (long-lived balances), **revenue recognised over time** by progress (IFRS 15) rather than on invoicing, **foreign currency** for imports, **withholding tax and VAT**, **fixed assets** such as plant, and **several legal entities** whose mutual charges must disappear when the group is consolidated.

Amounts are exact decimals in whole minor units (cents/santim): never floating-point.

@term Journal = A balanced set of debit and credit lines recording a financial event.
@term Posting = Making a journal permanent in the ledger.
@term Reversal = A new journal that cancels an earlier posted journal; the original is never altered.
@term Accounting period = A month or similar slice of the financial year that is open for posting or closed.
@term Subledger = Detailed ledger (payables, receivables, stock, fixed assets) that must equal one control account in the general ledger.
@term Remeasurement = Revaluing foreign-currency balances at the period-end rate (IAS 21).
@term Contract asset / liability = IFRS 15 balances showing revenue earned but not billed (asset) or billed but not earned (liability).
@term Intercompany = Transactions between group companies, eliminated on consolidation.

@case T-FIN-01 | Unbalanced, closed-period, fractional and self-approved journals are refused; corrections are by reversal | R1 | FIN-01, FIN-03, NFR-DAT-03, FIN-T01 | FIN
1. As fin.a prepare a journal with debits 100,000 and credits 99,000 and submit.
2. Prepare a balanced journal dated in closed period P0 and submit.
3. Enter an amount with a third decimal (100.005) on a line.
4. Prepare a valid journal of 250,000 (debit 5100 cost, credit 2100 payables), approve with fin.b, post with fin.c.
5. Try to edit and delete the posted journal. Then reverse it.
=> Step 1 refused: debits and credits differ by 1,000.00; step 2 refused: period closed; step 3 refused or rounded exactly per the stated rounding rule, never stored with hidden fractions.
=> Step 4 posts; the preparer, approver and poster are three different people.
=> Step 5: edit and delete are impossible; the reversal creates a second journal linked to the original; both appear in the ledger and net to zero.
note: Inspection evidence (R1): the automated suite shows the same refusals when attempted directly in the database, as tenant login and as schema owner.

@case T-FIN-03 | Accounting periods: closed periods refuse posting; reopening is privileged and audited | R1 | FIN-03, AUD-05 | FIN, CFO, AUD
1. Leave one mandatory reconciliation open (for example the bank reconciliation). As fin.c try to close P1.
2. Complete the reconciliations and close P1 (see UC-10). Then try to post a journal dated in P1.
3. As fin.c try to reopen P1; then as cfo.a reopen it with a reason and fresh MFA.
4. Post the journal; close P1 again.
=> Close is refused while mandatory reconciliations are open; the message lists them.
=> Posting into a closed period is refused. Reopen by fin.c is refused; the CFO reopening is accepted and appears in the control review report (who, why, how long it was open, what was posted).

@case T-FIN-04 | Multi-currency with approved rates and period-end remeasurement | R1 | FIN-04, ORG-01 | FIN, AP
pre: Approved FX policy: rate selected by transaction date. Rates: 120.00 on the invoice date, 125.00 on the payment date. Month-end closing rate 126.00.
1. Enter a USD 10,000 supplier invoice dated at rate 120. Pay it later at rate 125.
2. Enter another USD 5,000 invoice at 120.00 and leave it unpaid at month-end.
3. Run period-end remeasurement.
4. Try to enter a transaction using a manually typed rate that is not an approved rate.
=> Invoice books ETB 1,200,000. Payment 1,250,000 settles it; **realised FX loss 50,000**.
=> The open USD 5,000 payable (booked 600,000) is revalued at 126 = 630,000; **unrealised loss 30,000**, reversed automatically next period.
=> Step 4 is refused unless the rate is approved under the FX policy.

@case T-FIN-05 | AP and AR subledgers with ageing, reconciled to control accounts | R1 | FIN-05 | AP, FIN

@case T-FIN-06 | Fixed assets: acquisition, components, depreciation, transfer, revaluation, impairment and disposal | R2 | FIN-06, EQP-01 | FIN, EQP
pre: EX-01 cost 6,000,000, residual 600,000, 5-year straight line, in service this month.
1. Capitalise EX-01 with a component (engine 1,000,000, life 3 years) and the body (5,000,000, life 5 years).
2. Run depreciation for the month.
3. After 12 months of depreciation dispose of EX-01 for 4,000,000.
4. Test transfer to entity E1 and an impairment of 500,000 on a copy.
=> Whole-asset monthly depreciation (without the component split) = (6,000,000 - 600,000) / 60 = **90,000**; with components each is depreciated over its life; total is shown in the register.
=> After 12 months net book value = 6,000,000 - 1,080,000 = **4,920,000**; disposal at 4,000,000 gives **loss 920,000**.
=> Transfer, revaluation and impairment create journals with the required approvals; history is retained.
note: For the component split the finance lead gives the expected figures from the signed policy.
