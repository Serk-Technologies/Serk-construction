# 6. Organisation and master data (SRS 6.2)
@release R1

## Industry context
**Master data** is the list of "things" every transaction refers to: companies, projects, suppliers, customers, cost codes, materials. In a contractor the quality of master data decides the quality of every report: a project with the wrong client, a supplier with two records, or a cost code that is not in the approved structure produces numbers nobody can trust. Master data is also where fraud starts (a fake supplier, a changed bank account), so it is controlled at least as tightly as a payment.

@term Legal entity = A separately registered company within the group, with its own currency, tax registration and books.
@term Master data = Reference records (projects, suppliers, customers, cost codes, resources) that transactions point to.
@term TIN = Tax identification number of a business partner.
@term Pre-qualification = Checking that a supplier or subcontractor is licensed, insured and tax-compliant before use.

@case T-ORG-01 | The group is modelled with entities, regions, business units and projects | R1 | ORG-01 | FIN, ADM
1. Open the organisation structure. Confirm E1 Abay Builders PLC and E2 Abay Plant and Logistics PLC exist.
2. Open each entity's settings. Compare functional currency, tax registration, chart-of-accounts mapping and fiscal calendar.
3. Create a region "Addis Ababa" and a business unit "Residential"; assign project P-101 to both.
4. Change E2's fiscal calendar start and confirm E1 is unaffected.
=> Each entity holds its own currency, tax registration, chart mapping and fiscal calendar, editable independently.
=> The project belongs to exactly one legal entity and may be tagged with region and business unit.
=> Changes to master data are audited with before and after values.
why: A group often has a plant-hire company, a property company and joint ventures with different tax numbers; mixing their books breaks statutory reporting.

@case T-ORG-02 | The project master carries everything a project needs, with stage-gate history | R1 | ORG-02 | PM, QS
1. Open project P-101. Check code, name, client, contract type, location with GPS coordinates, key dates, project manager, phase and status.
2. Move the phase from "Tender" to "Mobilisation" through the stage gate. Enter a reason.
3. Open the stage-gate history.
=> All listed fields exist and are validated (for example GPS coordinates must be valid latitude/longitude).
=> The phase change is recorded with user, date and reason; earlier phases are still visible and cannot be edited.

@case T-ORG-03 | New suppliers need approval; bank accounts are dual-approved, masked and reveal is audited | R1 | ORG-03, IAM-08, AUD-01 | PROC, AP, TRS, AUD
1. As proc.a create supplier S-05 "Blue Nile Paints PLC" with a bank account. Submit.
2. As proc.a try to approve it. Then as proc.b approve.
3. Open the supplier as ap.a. Look at the bank account number.
4. As a user holding the reveal permission (trs.a) press Reveal; give a reason.
5. As proc.b block the supplier, then try to raise a purchase order to it.
=> Self-approval is refused (step 2). The bank account needs its own approval by a second user with fresh MFA before it can be used for payment.
=> Step 3 shows the account masked (for example last four digits only). Step 4 reveals it and writes an audit record with who, when and the reason.
=> A blocked supplier cannot receive purchase orders; the screen says the supplier is blocked and by whom.
why: Substituting a bank account just before a payment run is the most common supplier fraud.

@case T-ORG-04 | The customer master holds TIN, credit limit and terms, and is approved | R1 | ORG-04 | AP, FIN
1. As ap.a create customer "Zenith Properties PLC" with TIN, credit limit 150,000,000 and payment terms 30 days. Submit.
2. Try to approve as ap.a, then as ap.b.
3. Create a second customer with the same TIN.
=> Self-approval refused; second user approves.
=> All fields are mandatory where the SRS lists them (TIN, limit, terms); the screen explains missing fields.
=> Step 3: see T-ORG-08 for the duplicate warning.

@case T-ORG-05 | Supplier and subcontractor pre-qualification with expiry alerts | R1 | ORG-05, SUB-04 | PROC, QA
pre: Supplier S-05 has an insurance certificate expiring in 25 days and a tax-clearance certificate that has already expired.
1. Open S-05 pre-qualification: trade licence, contractor grade, tax clearance, VAT registration, insurance with expiry dates.
2. Wait for or trigger the expiry alert job.
3. Record a performance rating after a delivery (price, time, quality).
=> Expired documents are clearly marked. An alert (notification) is raised before expiry at the configured lead time (record it).
=> The performance rating is stored with its inputs and visible when choosing suppliers.

@case T-ORG-06 | Cost codes and the WBS are versioned and approved; documents use only approved versions | R1 | ORG-06, EST-07 | PM, FIN, PROC
1. As pm.a draft a new WBS version for P-101 adding code 04-200 "External plaster".
2. Before approval, try to raise a requisition on 04-200.
3. As pm.a try to approve the version; then as qs.a approve it.
4. Raise the requisition on 04-200 again.
=> Step 2 is refused: only approved versions can be referenced.
=> Step 3: self-approval refused; second approver succeeds. The old version remains visible in history.
=> Step 4 succeeds.
why: If anyone can invent a cost code, costs get hidden in codes nobody monitors.

@case T-ORG-07 | The resource library knows units and conversions | R1 | ORG-07, INV-04 | PROC, STORE, EST
1. Open the resource library. Check labour trades, plant types, materials and subcontract packages.
2. Open "Cement OPC": purchase unit bag, base unit kg, 1 bag = 50 kg; 20 bags = 1 tonne.
3. Try to save a resource with a unit that has no conversion to the base unit while another document needs it.
=> The conversion factors are stored with the resource and used everywhere (see inventory chapter).
=> Step 3 is refused with an explanation of which conversion is missing.

@case T-ORG-08 | Probable duplicate partners are flagged for review | R1 | ORG-08 | PROC, AP
1. Create supplier "Nile Cement Distribution Plc" (note: similar name) with the same TIN as S-01.
2. Create a supplier with a new name but S-01's bank account number.
=> Both are saved as drafts but flagged as probable duplicates with the matching reason (TIN, bank account or similar name) and routed to a reviewer.
=> The flagged supplier cannot be used for orders until the reviewer clears or merges it.

@case T-ORG-09 | Working calendars include Ethiopian holidays, shifts and non-working days | R1 | ORG-09 | PM, HR
1. Open the working calendar for E1 and for P-101.
2. Check Ethiopian public holidays for the year (for example Meskel, Enkutatash, Timkat) appear as non-working.
3. Define a shift (06:00-14:00) and a site non-working day (rain day).
4. Schedule an activity of 5 working days starting on the Thursday before a holiday weekend.
=> Holidays and non-working days are excluded from durations; the activity finish date moves accordingly.
=> Project calendar overrides the entity calendar; changes are audited.
note: The list of public holidays must be confirmed by the client (it changes yearly, including movable religious holidays).
