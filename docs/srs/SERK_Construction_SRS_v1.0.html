1. IntroductionQQs1-introductionQQ

1.1 PurposeQQs1-1-purposeQQ

This document specifies the functional and non-functional requirements of SERK Construction OS, an enterprise resource planning platform for construction contractors. It covers the whole contractor lifecycle: winning work, controlling project cost and time, buying and subcontracting, running sites, paying people and suppliers, billing clients, and closing the books. It is the single reference against which design, implementation, testing and acceptance are measured, and it sets the scope needed to compete with established international construction and ERP suites.

1.2 ScopeQQs1-2-scopeQQ

In scope: multi-company, multi-project construction operations: organisation and master data; business development, estimating and tendering; project controls (budget, commitment, cost, forecast, WIP); planning, scheduling and earned value; procurement and import; subcontract management; inventory; equipment and fleet; workforce, time and payroll; client contracts, variations, claims and revenue; finance, tax, fixed assets and consolidation; treasury; site operations and an offline mobile application; document control and BIM; quality; health, safety and environment; workflow and approvals; reporting and analytics; governed AI; audit; portals; integration and administration.

Out of scope for release 1.x (unless added by change control): design authoring (CAD/BIM modelling), real-estate sales and property management, manufacturing (precast plant MRP), full recruitment and talent management, e-commerce, and direct movement of money (the system issues instructions and reconciles; banks settle).

2. Overall DescriptionQQs2-overall-descriptionQQ

2.1 Product perspectiveQQs2-1-product-perspectiveQQ

SERK Construction OS is a multi-tenant modular monolith written in Go on PostgreSQL, the authoritative store and last line of defence. Business commands pass through a kernel that checks permission, project scope and segregation of duties, then commit the change, its audit record and its outbox event in one transaction. An isolated approval signer holds the attestation key. Separate processes deliver outbox events (relay), run migrations and serve the web console. Users reach the system through a browser console, an offline-capable mobile application and external portals over HTTPS. Integrations use a versioned REST API and signed webhooks.

2.4 Operating environmentQQs2-4-operating-environmentQQ

  • Server: Linux containers; Go services (API, signer, relay, migrator, workers); PostgreSQL 16+ with point-in-time recovery; S3-compatible object storage; secret vault and HSM/KMS; TLS edge with WAF.
  • Deployment models: multi-tenant SaaS, dedicated single-tenant, in-country hosting, and on-premises including air-gapped.
  • Clients: current desktop browsers; tablets and phones; Android (priority) and iOS mobile app; intermittent connectivity on site must be expected.

2.5 Design and implementation constraintsQQs2-5-design-and-implementation-constraintsQQ

  • The server is the authority for every amount, status and permission; the client holds none.
  • Controls are enforced twice: in the kernel and by PostgreSQL (constraints, triggers, RLS, attestation checks).
  • Money is exact decimal; migrations are additive; posted records are never edited or deleted.
  • No AI, automation or service principal holds decision authority.
  • Payments are instructions only; settlement comes from bank evidence.

3.1 Authoritative write pathQQs3-1-authoritative-write-pathQQ

The API authenticates the principal and decodes the request. The kernel checks permission, project scope, segregation of duties and business rules, obtains an attestation from the isolated signer for human decisions, and executes one tenant transaction. PostgreSQL then re-verifies the attestation, state machine, balances, periods, budget and tenant identity, and commits the document, its journal (if any), the hash-chained audit record and the outbox event together or not at all. Notifications, webhooks, analytics and AI happen only after commit.

12. Quality Assurance ApproachQQs12-quality-assurance-approachQQ

  • Requirements trace to design, code, tests and evidence in a matrix maintained for the life of the product (docs/release/FEATURE_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.csv).
  • CI runs unit, integration (fresh PostgreSQL), race, security-probe, migration and browser-journey tests on every change.
  • Defects are classified Critical, High, Medium or Low; no Critical or High defect may be open at release.
  • Changes to financial, approval, stock, payroll, contract or sync modules require review by two engineers, and an independent review before each release.
  • Gate status is generated from evidence files (tools/gate_status.py), never written by hand.

13. Commercial and Support RequirementsQQs13-commercial-and-support-requirementsQQ

  • COM-01: Support tiers with defined response times (Critical: 1 hour, 24x7 for premium).
  • COM-02: A published roadmap, release notes, and deprecation of any API version with at least 12 months' notice.

  • COM-04: Training material and in-product help in every supported language.

14. Change ControlQQs14-change-controlQQ

  • After sign-off, every addition, deletion or modification of a requirement is raised as a written change request stating the reason, the affected IDs, and the impact on controls, compliance, schedule and cost.
  • Changes to Must requirements or to invariants R1-R9 require approval by the Product Owner, the Technical Lead and the Finance/Compliance Lead.
  • Approved changes increment the document version and are recorded in the revision history. Requirement IDs are never reused.
